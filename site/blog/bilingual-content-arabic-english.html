Arabic and English content: how bilingual brands plan social media

The UAE is one of the most multilingual markets in the world, and choosing how to handle Arabic and English is a real strategic decision. There isn't one right answer, but there are clear trade-offs.

Do you need Arabic?

Look at who your customers are. Brands serving Emirati and Arab audiences, families, government-facing services and many consumer categories benefit from Arabic content. Some B2B and expat-focused businesses can work mainly in English. The answer should come from your own data, not from a general rule.

Where to find the answer

  • The language of the DMs, WhatsApp messages and emails you already receive.
  • The language of your Google reviews and the questions people ask on your profile.
  • Language settings in your website analytics.
  • What your best customers say when you simply ask them.

If a meaningful share of these signals are in Arabic, you're already losing something by not answering in Arabic. If almost none are, start with greetings and key campaigns and see if demand grows.

Three ways to do it

For most small and medium businesses, I start with combined posts for important announcements and alternating posts for everyday content. Separate accounts make sense once each audience is large enough to justify its own calendar, community management and reporting.

Tone and copy

The biggest mistake is treating Arabic as a translation job at the end of the process. Good bilingual brands write for each language, starting from the same brief.

  • Write Arabic natively, or have a native speaker adapt it. Don't translate word for word.
  • Decide between Modern Standard Arabic and Gulf dialect based on your audience and brand. Formal services often suit standard Arabic; playful consumer brands may suit dialect.
  • Hashtags and keywords differ by language. Research them separately.
  • Check humour, idioms and references. What's clever in English can be confusing or inappropriate in Arabic.

Design for right-to-left

  • Arabic reads right to left. Mirror layouts where text direction matters, including arrows and step sequences.
  • Choose Arabic and Latin fonts designed to work together, so the two languages feel like one brand.
  • Leave room: Arabic and English text often take different amounts of space.
  • For video, plan subtitles in both languages and keep them clear of on-screen buttons.

Paid ads in two languages

On ad platforms, target by language where you can and run separate ads for each language rather than one ad with both. That way you can see which language drives results and adjust budgets. The same principle applies to landing pages: an Arabic ad should lead to an Arabic page.

Community management in both languages

Posting in Arabic creates an expectation that you'll reply in Arabic. Before you start, make sure someone can answer comments and DMs in both languages within your normal response time. If that person is part-time, set saved replies for common questions in Arabic and English, and agree how complex enquiries are handed over. When you review results each month, look at engagement and enquiries by language, so you can see whether the Arabic content is reaching the people it was made for.
