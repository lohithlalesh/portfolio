How Google decides who shows up on the map in Dubai

When someone in Dubai searches "dentist near me", Google shows a map with a short list of businesses above the usual results. Getting into that list is worth more than almost any other SEO win for a local business. Here's how Google decides, based on what it actually publishes rather than on rumours.

What Google says

Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity.

That's from Google's own help page, which then explains each factor under the headings relevance, distance and prominence. Under prominence it adds that it uses information like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. It also states there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

That short page is more useful than most ranking-factor lists online, because it tells you what Google is trying to reward. Everything else is a way of feeding those three factors with accurate information.

Relevance: match the search

Relevance is where most quick gains are. Google says it wants complete and detailed business information so it can match you to searches. In practice that means three things.

  • Choose the most specific primary category. "Pediatric dentist" beats "Dentist" if that's what you are.
  • List every service in the profile and on your website.
  • Use the words your customers use. If they search "AC repair", don't only say "HVAC maintenance".

In a bilingual market, also think about the words Arabic-speaking customers use. If a large share of your customers search in Arabic, a proper Arabic version of your site helps more than English pages stuffed with transliterations. I cover that in Arabic SEO and bilingual websites.

Distance: work with it, don't fight it

Dubai is spread out, and Google weighs how close you are to the person searching. A business in Deira won't usually rank in the map for someone searching in Dubai Marina. That's why fake addresses are tempting, and why they get profiles suspended. The honest approach is to rank strongly near where you really are, and use your website and ads to reach further.

One Dubai-specific detail: check that your pin is in the right place. In towers and large developments, the default pin can land on the wrong building or the wrong side of a road. If Google thinks you're somewhere else, both your distance and your directions are wrong.

Prominence: become known

Prominence is the slow factor. It reflects how well known you are online and offline, and you build it by being a business people talk about and link to.

  • Reviews: steady, genuine reviews over time. Google's policies forbid incentivised ones. See how to get more Google reviews in the UAE.
  • Links and mentions: local press, partner websites, associations and event pages that mention you.
  • Website strength: a well-built site with useful content supports your profile.
  • Consistency: the same name, address and phone number everywhere. More in NAP consistency.

Why you might rank in one area and not another

Rankings on the map change from street to street, because distance is part of the calculation. When you check your ranking, search from different locations or use a tool that checks a grid of points, rather than judging from your own office. Searching from your desk will almost always flatter you.

How I check map visibility

List the searches

Five to ten searches real customers use, in English and Arabic if relevant.

Pick the points

Your own area, two neighbouring areas and one further away.

Search logged out

Use a private window or a grid tool, so your history does not skew results.

Record and repeat

Note who appears and repeat monthly, rather than daily.

What doesn't move the map

  • Paying for Google Ads. Ads can appear on Maps, but they're labelled and don't change the organic list.
  • Buying reviews or offering discounts for them. Those reviews get removed and put the profile at risk.
  • Keywords in your business name. It may seem to work for a while, then it's a suspension risk.
  • Posting every day for the sake of it. Posts help customers; they're not a ranking shortcut.

Where to start

If you're not in the map for searches near your own premises, the problem is almost always relevance or a broken profile, not prominence. Work through the local SEO checklist first, then look at reviews and links.
