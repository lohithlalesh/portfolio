Landing page or full website? Which brings more leads

A landing page does one job: turn a specific visitor into a lead. A website does many jobs. Which you need depends on where your visitors come from and what you want them to do.

When someone asks me whether they need a landing page or a website, I don't start with design. I ask where the visitors will come from. Someone who clicked a Google ad for "AC repair JLT" needs something very different from someone who found you on Google Maps or heard your name from a friend. Get the destination right for the traffic and the decision mostly makes itself.

Side by side

How they differ

Full website

  • Many pages, many purposes
  • Builds search visibility over time
  • Navigation lets visitors explore
  • Better for trust and research

Landing page

  • One page, one offer, one action
  • Built for ad traffic or campaigns
  • Minimal navigation, fewer distractions
  • Faster and cheaper to build and test

Start with the traffic source

Each source arrives with a different level of intent and a different question in mind. This is the mapping I use when planning a campaign or a new site.

Choose a landing page when...

A landing page wins when the visitor already knows roughly what they want and you need them to act on one specific offer.

  • You're running ads for one specific offer.
  • You're launching something and need a page fast.
  • Budget is tight and you need enquiries before a full site.
  • You want to test messages or prices quickly.

Choose a website when...

A website wins when people need to compare, research or find you through search over months rather than days.

  • You want to rank for several services in Google.
  • Customers research before buying and want to explore.
  • You need to build long-term trust and authority.
  • You're in a regulated sector and need room for licences, credentials and policies.

What a good landing page includes

The structure is simple. The discipline is in leaving things out.

  1. A headline that matches the ad people clicked.
  2. The offer and who it's for, in plain language.
  3. Proof: reviews, results, logos, certifications.
  4. Answers to the top objections: price, time, process.
  5. One clear action, repeated down the page.
  6. Fast loading on mobile.

Google Ads also rates each keyword's landing page experience, looking at whether the page is clear and useful and whether its content matches the keyword and what people searched for. A page written for one ad group is far easier to keep relevant than a homepage that has to speak to every visitor at once.

Mistakes I see with landing pages in Dubai

  • Full site navigation left in, so visitors wander off to the blog.
  • A headline that's the brand slogan rather than the offer from the ad.
  • English-only pages behind Arabic ads, or the reverse. See bilingual content.
  • Long forms that ask for budget, company size and a message before the visitor knows the price range.
  • No tracking on the WhatsApp button, so the page looks like it isn't working when it is.
  • Campaign pages left live and indexed after the offer ends, confusing anyone who finds them later.

For short-lived campaign pages I usually add a noindex tag, so they don't compete in search with the main service pages they were copied from.

How to tell if it is working

Compare cost per lead, and more importantly, the share of leads that become real conversations. Compare like with like: an ad landing page and your organic website serve different visitors, so their conversion rates tell you little side by side. Set up tracking first with GA4 and Meta conversion tracking, and if the leads arrive but don't answer the phone, read fixing low-quality leads.

A quick way to decide

List your traffic sources

Ads, search, Maps, social, referrals. Note which ones you'll actually use in the next three months.

Count your offers

One offer suits a landing page. Several services usually need a website.

Check your timeline

If you need enquiries within weeks, start with a landing page and build the site behind it.

Build the smallest thing that works

Launch it, measure it, then add pages where the data says people need more.

Often the answer is both

A website for search and trust, plus dedicated landing pages for each ad campaign. I build both: see website design in Dubai.
