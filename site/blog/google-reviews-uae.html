How to get more Google reviews in the UAE without breaking the rules

Reviews are one of the few local ranking factors you can actively build. But Google's rules are strict, and some common tactics in Dubai, like discounts for reviews, can get reviews removed. Here's how to grow them the right way, starting with what's off-limits so you don't waste effort.

What Google doesn't allow

Google's policy on fake engagement is specific. It says businesses must not:

  • Offer incentives, such as payment, discounts, free goods or services, in exchange for a review, or for changing or removing a negative one.
  • Discourage negative reviews or selectively ask only happy customers for reviews.
  • Pressure customers to leave reviews while on the premises, or ask them to include specific content.
  • Set staff quotas for collecting reviews.

Reviews that break these rules are removed, and repeated violations can affect your profile. Google's own review tips page calls incentives in exchange for reviews "strictly prohibited".

Side by side

Allowed or not

Breaks the rules

  • 10% off your next visit for a review
  • Only sending the link after a 5-star survey
  • Asking staff to collect a set number each week
  • Telling customers what to write

Within the rules

  • Asking every customer the same way
  • Sending the direct review link after the job
  • A QR code at the counter, with no pressure
  • Asking for an honest review

The one that catches most businesses is review gating: sending a satisfaction survey first, then only inviting the happy people to Google. Many review tools are built to do exactly this, so check the settings of anything you use.

What works instead

The businesses I see with steady review growth don't run campaigns. They make asking a normal part of finishing a job, the same way they send an invoice.

A simple review system

Ask everyone

Make asking part of the process for every customer, not just the happy ones.

Make it easy

Use the direct review link from your Business Profile, in a WhatsApp message, email or a QR code at the counter.

Time it well

Ask right after the moment of value: delivery, a finished treatment, a completed project.

Reply to all

Thank people by name and respond calmly to criticism.

One practical point: Google says customers must be signed in to a Google account to leave a review. That's rarely a problem on Android, but some iPhone users aren't signed in. Sending the link by WhatsApp or email, so they can open it later on a device where they are signed in, removes a lot of friction.

A message template

Thanks again for choosing us. If you have a minute, we'd really value an honest review on Google. It helps other people in Dubai find us: [link]

Notice the word "honest". You're asking for a genuine experience, which is what the policy wants. If many of your customers prefer Arabic, send an Arabic version too. Reviews in the language your customers search in are useful to the next person reading them.

Replying to reviews

Replies are public, and potential customers read them more closely than the reviews themselves. A calm, specific reply to a bad review often does more for you than another five-star rating.

Negative reviews

  1. Reply within a few days, calmly and briefly.
  2. Acknowledge the experience without arguing details in public.
  3. Never share personal details, booking information or medical details in a reply.
  4. Move the conversation offline: a phone number or email.
  5. If you fix the issue, the customer may update their review on their own. Don't ask them to in exchange for anything.

Positive reviews

Keep it short and human. Thank them by name, mention the specific thing they liked if you can, and stop there. Copy-pasting the same reply to every review looks automated and adds nothing.

Is it working

Look at the trend over months, not weeks. A steady flow of reviews, a few each month, reads as more trustworthy than a sudden burst followed by silence, and it's what an honest routine naturally produces. Also read what people say. Recurring complaints about parking, waiting times or finding the entrance are things to fix in the business, not only in the reply. Fake reviews

If you see reviews you believe are fake, you can report them through your Business Profile, and Google reviews them against its content policies. Don't respond with fake positive reviews of your own.
