Hiring one marketing expert for a small company in the UAE

Most small companies in the UAE will only ever have one marketing person at a time, whether that is an employee, a freelancer or a part-time senior. The question is what that one person can carry, and how to set them up so it adds up to results.

When a small company decides to "get a marketing person", the decision usually comes down to a name rather than a role. Someone gets hired because they were recommended or because their portfolio looked good, and then they are asked to do everything. Three months later the owner is disappointed and the marketer is exhausted. Usually neither of them did anything wrong. The job was never defined.

"It's not about freelancer or employee. It's about the individual: what they will be able to contribute." Lohith Lalesh

That is how I think about it. Whether the person is on your payroll or invoices you, the useful question is the same: what will this one person own, and what will they need help with?

What one person can realistically own

One capable marketer can run a surprising amount, as long as the work is planned and the channels are few. What breaks a one-person setup is volume and variety at the same time.

Side by side

One marketing expert, honestly

Hard for one person to carry alone

  • Five or more channels posting at once
  • Original video production every week
  • Native Arabic and English copy plus design plus ads
  • Instant replies to every DM and WhatsApp all day
  • Strategy, execution and reporting with no review time

Realistic for one person to own

  • A clear plan for one goal and two or three channels
  • Google Business Profile, website updates and basic SEO
  • One paid channel with tracking set up properly
  • A steady content rhythm using footage you supply
  • A monthly report tied to enquiries or sales

The right side is not a small job. Done consistently, it can be enough for a small service business to see steady enquiries. The left side is where you either add a second pair of hands or accept that something will be done less often.

Generalist or specialist first

The usual advice is "hire a generalist first". I think that's right for most small companies, with one condition: the generalist needs to be strong in the channel closest to your money. A generalist who is excellent at social content but weak on tracking and ads will keep you visible without making you measurable.

How to set them up to contribute

Most of the value from one marketing person is decided in the first two weeks, by how they are set up. These are the steps I'd follow on either side of the table.

Hiring a marketer · Dubai

Setting up a one-person marketing function

Write one goal

One number for the next 90 days: enquiries, bookings, sales or sign-ups. Not followers. A one-page marketing brief is enough.

Give access, keep ownership

Add them as a user to your Google, Meta and analytics accounts. Domains, ad accounts and pages stay in the company's name.

Fund the tools and production

Budget separately for ad spend, software, stock and any shoots, so their time goes on marketing, not on finding workarounds.

Agree what they will not do

Write down the channels and tasks that are out of scope this quarter. It protects their focus and your expectations.

Review monthly, decide quarterly

A short monthly review of results, and a bigger decision every quarter about what to add, cut or hand to a specialist.

If you're starting from nothing, my 90-day marketing plan for a new Dubai business shows what a sensible first quarter looks like for one person.

Signs one person is no longer enough

  • Good work is happening, but always late, because there is more of it than hours.
  • One channel is clearly working and could take more budget, but nobody has time to scale it.
  • Your marketer is spending more time on design or editing than on planning and reporting.
  • Enquiries are coming in faster than anyone replies to them.

When you see these, the next step is usually not a second generalist. It is a specialist for the bottleneck: a designer, an editor, an ads manager or a few senior hours a month. The one-person setup did its job; it has just been outgrown.
