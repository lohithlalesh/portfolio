Google Ads for Dubai businesses: a setup guide that avoids wasted spend

Most wasted Google Ads budget I see in Dubai comes from a handful of default settings and missing tracking, not from bad ads. Fix these before you spend a dirham.

Google Ads is the one paid channel where people tell you exactly what they want. Someone typing "AC repair JLT" or "corporate lawyer DIFC" has a problem today. That intent is why search ads can work so well for Dubai service businesses, and also why a badly set up account burns money so fast: you pay for every click, including the ones from people who were never going to buy.

This guide is the order I set up an account in. It is written for small and mid-sized businesses running their own Search campaigns, or checking the work of whoever runs them.

Step one is tracking, not keywords

Set up conversion tracking first: form submissions, calls, WhatsApp clicks or purchases. Without it, Google's bidding has nothing to learn from, and you can't tell which keywords bring customers. In the UAE, a large share of enquiries happen on WhatsApp or the phone, so a setup that only counts form fills will undercount your best campaigns. See conversion tracking with GA4 and Meta for the full stack.

If you collect email addresses or phone numbers in your forms, look at Google's enhanced conversions. It sends hashed customer data with the conversion so Google can match more of them to ad clicks, which helps when cookies are blocked.

Settings that waste money

These are the defaults I change on almost every account I audit. None of them look dangerous in the interface, which is the problem.

  • Location set to "presence or interest". This can show ads to people outside the UAE who are merely interested in Dubai. For local services, choose "presence" (people in or regularly in your locations).
  • Search partners and Display network ticked on a Search campaign. Start with Google Search only, then test partners separately so you can see their results on their own.
  • Broad match with no negative keywords. You'll pay for loosely related searches. Add negatives from day one and review the search terms report weekly.
  • Auto-applied recommendations. Review them before allowing changes to your account. Some raise budgets or add keywords without you noticing.
  • Ad schedule left at 24/7 when nobody answers the phone at 2am. If leads need a quick reply, run ads in the hours you can respond.

A simple structure that stays manageable

Small accounts get into trouble when they copy the structure of a big one. You don't need twenty campaigns. You need a structure where each ad clearly matches the search and the page it leads to.

Campaign structure for a service business

One campaign per goal

For example, one for your main service, one for brand searches. Separate budgets stop one eating the other.

Tight ad groups

Group keywords by theme so ads and landing pages match the search.

Matching landing pages

Send each ad group to the most relevant page, not the homepage.

Clear ads

Say what you do, where, and why choose you. Include a call to action.

The landing page matters as much as the ad. A page that loads slowly on mobile, hides the phone number or asks for ten form fields will waste good clicks. If you don't have a page per service, a focused landing page is often the cheapest fix.

Keywords in a bilingual market

Many UAE searches are in English, but some categories see significant Arabic searching. Test Arabic keywords with Arabic ads if your audience uses them, and send them to an Arabic page. Don't put English ads on Arabic searches. My notes on keyword research for Dubai cover how to find what people actually type, including area names like Al Barsha, Business Bay or Mirdif that often signal a local buyer.

Negative keywords to add on day one

Every business has searches it never wants to pay for. Common ones I add straight away: jobs, careers, salary, free, course, training, and DIY or "how to" terms if you sell a service. Then add competitors' names you don't want, and locations you don't serve. After that, the search terms report tells you the rest.

Budget and bidding

Start with a budget you can sustain for at least a few weeks so the campaign has time to learn. Google treats your budget as an average daily amount: some days it spends more and some less, but over a month it won't charge more than the daily budget times 30.4, according to Google's own documentation. Plan around the monthly figure.

Begin with a bidding strategy that maximises conversions once tracking works, and review cost per lead rather than cost per click. A cheap click that never converts is the most expensive kind. More in how much a Dubai small business should spend on ads.

How to tell if it is working

Quality Score, shown per keyword, is a useful diagnostic. Low scores usually point to a mismatch between the keyword, the ad and the landing page. Fix the match rather than chasing the number.

The weekly 15 minutes

Check the search terms report, add negatives, pause what's clearly not working, and look at cost per conversion by ad group. That habit alone saves a lot of money.

If you're weighing search ads against organic work, I've compared the two in SEO vs Google Ads in Dubai. Most businesses end up using both: ads for immediate demand, SEO for the long run.
