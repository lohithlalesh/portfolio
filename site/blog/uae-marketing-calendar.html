UAE marketing calendar: the dates that move sales

Marketing in the UAE runs on a calendar that's different from most of the world. Islamic months move each year, the summer behaves differently, and some of the biggest retail moments are local. Here's the year at a glance, and how I turn it into a plan.

Why the UAE calendar needs its own plan

If you copy a European or American marketing calendar, you'll miss half the moments that matter here and push hard on some that barely register. Three things make the UAE different. The biggest religious occasions follow the lunar calendar, so they move every year. The summer is a quiet season for footfall in many categories because a lot of residents travel and the heat keeps people indoors. And Dubai runs its own citywide retail festivals, which shift where attention and ad money go.

The working week matters too. The UAE federal government moved to a Monday to Friday-morning week with a Saturday and Sunday weekend from 1 January 2022, and many private companies followed. That changes when people read emails, when B2B buyers are reachable and when weekend leisure content lands.

Dates that move each year

Ramadan, Eid Al Fitr and Eid Al Adha follow the Islamic lunar calendar, so they arrive about 10 to 11 days earlier each Gregorian year. Official dates are confirmed by moon sighting, so plan around projected dates and keep a few days of flexibility in anything you schedule.

For 2027, the UAE's Astronomy Centre projects Ramadan to begin around 8 February, with Eid Al Fitr expected on 9 or 10 March, subject to official moon-sighting confirmation, according to The National. Eid Al Adha falls on 10 Dhu Al Hijjah, roughly ten weeks after Eid Al Fitr, so expect it in spring 2027 and check projected dates once they're published. More detail in Ramadan marketing in the UAE.

The official public holiday list on u.ae also includes Hijri New Year (1 Muharram), the Prophet's birthday (12 Rabi Al Awwal) and Arafah Day. These are shorter breaks, but they still change working patterns and response times for a day or two.

How I turn the calendar into a plan

UAE calendar · Dubai

Building a 12-month calendar for one business

Mark the fixed and lunar dates

Add public holidays, projected Islamic dates and the festival windows from the table. Flag anything still unconfirmed.

Pick three to five moments

Choose the ones that match your customers and margin. A B2B consultancy and a perfume shop should not have the same list.

Work backwards six to eight weeks

Each moment gets a planning date, a creative deadline and an approval date, so nothing is rushed.

Fill the gaps with evergreen content

The weeks between big moments are where you build trust. Customer questions and proof of work fill them well.

Review after each moment

Note what sold, what it cost and what you would change. Next year's plan starts with these notes.

Budgets and ad costs through the year

Ad auctions get crowded around the biggest moments because large brands compete for the same attention. You don't need exact figures to plan for that. Start campaigns before the peak so your ads have learned and gathered data by the time competition rises, and keep some budget back for the final days, when intent is highest. In quieter months, test new offers and creative while it's cheaper to learn. My ad budget guide for Dubai covers how to split spend across a year.

Common calendar mistakes

  • Forcing every date. A National Day post with nothing to say can do more harm than skipping it.
  • Planning Ramadan from the Gregorian date of last year. It will be roughly eleven days earlier.
  • Ignoring summer. Fewer people are in the city, but those who stay are often easier and cheaper to reach.
  • Using festival logos or names in a way that implies an official partnership you don't have.
  • Forgetting operational updates: holiday hours on your Google Business Profile, auto-replies and staff cover.

Check before you publish

Every date in this calendar should be confirmed against an official source in the weeks before you use it. Moon sighting, festival announcements and holiday circulars can all shift plans by a day or more.
