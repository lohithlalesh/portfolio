Webflow vs WordPress vs Shopify for UAE businesses

The platform matters less than people think, and the wrong choice is usually the one your team can't update. Here's how the three most common options compare for UAE businesses.

Start with who will edit the site

Most platform debates focus on features. The question I ask first is who will log in on a Tuesday afternoon to change the opening hours or add a new service. If that person is a busy owner or an office manager, the best platform is the one they'll actually use without calling a developer. A site that nobody updates goes stale, and stale sites lose enquiries and rankings slowly enough that nobody notices until it's a problem.

Choose Webflow if...

You want a fast, design-led marketing site that your team can edit visually, without worrying about plugins and updates. Hosting, security and backups are handled by the platform, and the editor lets non-technical staff change text and images without breaking the layout.

Watch out for

Plan limits on CMS items, form submissions and seats, which can push you to a higher tier as the site grows. Webflow offers a paid Localization feature for multilingual sites with per-locale URLs and styling. For Arabic, test a right-to-left layout of your actual design before committing, because mirrored layouts usually need extra styling work on any platform. Complex stores with many variants or custom checkout rules are usually better on Shopify.

Choose WordPress if...

You publish a lot of content, need specific plugins, or want full control of hosting. WordPress has the largest choice of themes and plugins, mature multilingual plugins for Arabic and English, and WooCommerce if you want to add a store to an existing content site.

Watch out for

Budget for regular updates and security. Every plugin is code someone else maintains, and an outdated one is the most common way small WordPress sites get hacked. Cheap shared hosting often makes WordPress slow, so the hosting choice matters as much as the theme. Decide up front who applies updates and who restores the site if an update breaks something.

Choose Shopify if...

Selling products online is the main job. Product management, checkout, inventory and order handling are the core of the platform, and the app store covers most extras.

Watch out for

Check payment gateway, delivery and cash-on-delivery options available in the UAE for your setup before you build, and confirm your trade licence fits the provider's requirements. Apps add monthly costs that are easy to underestimate, and some slow the storefront. If you plan to offer buy-now-pay-later, check that your preferred providers integrate with your platform. More on the commercial side in e-commerce marketing in the UAE.

Arabic and bilingual sites

If you need Arabic now or later, decide early. Retrofitting a right-to-left version onto a site designed only for English is slower than planning both from the start. Each language should have its own URLs, proper translations rather than machine output, and hreflang tags so Google shows the right version. The details are in Arabic SEO for bilingual websites.

Compare total cost, not the monthly fee

The headline subscription is only part of the bill. When I compare platforms for a client, I list every recurring cost over the first few years.

  • Platform subscription or hosting.
  • Premium themes, templates and plugins or apps, many of which renew yearly.
  • Transaction or payment fees for stores.
  • Developer time for updates, fixes and new pages.
  • Localization or multilingual add-ons.

What matters more than the platform

  • Who will update the site, and how comfortable they are with the tool.
  • Speed on mobile. See Core Web Vitals explained.
  • Ownership: the domain and accounts in your name.
  • Whether you need Arabic now or later.

Ownership deserves a second mention. A common problem is a business that can't edit its own site because a former agency holds the domain, the hosting and the admin login. Whatever you choose, register the domain and the platform account in the company's name and add the developer as a user.

Switching platforms later

Moving is common and manageable if you plan it. Map every old URL to its new equivalent with 301 redirects, keep the titles and content of pages that already rank, and submit the new sitemap in Search Console. Google's own guidance on site moves walks through the process, and my technical SEO checklist covers what to check afterwards.

How I pick a platform with a client

Define the main job

Leads, content or online sales. One platform usually fits the main job better.

Name the editor

The person who will update the site, and what they're comfortable using.

List must-haves

Arabic, bookings, payments, integrations with your CRM or delivery partner.

Cost it over three years

Subscription, apps, plugins and developer time, not just month one.

I build on all three, so I don't have a reason to push one. If you want a recommendation for your situation, see website design in Dubai.
