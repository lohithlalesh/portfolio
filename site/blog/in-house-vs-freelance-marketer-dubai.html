In-house marketer or freelancer: what a Dubai SME should choose

A full-time hire feels like commitment to growth. Sometimes it is. But for many small businesses in Dubai, the real cost of an employee is much higher than the salary, and one person rarely covers every skill you need.

The real cost of an in-house hire

In the UAE, an employee's cost goes well beyond their monthly salary. Depending on the arrangement, it can include visa and onboarding costs, health insurance, end-of-service gratuity, annual leave, equipment and the software they need. Then there's the time it takes you to recruit and manage them.

Gratuity is the one owners most often leave out of the sums. Under UAE rules for the private sector, an employee who completes a year of service is entitled to 21 days' basic salary for each of the first five years, and 30 days' for each year after that, capped at two years' wages. It's paid at the end, but it builds up from the first year, so it belongs in the monthly cost.

The skills problem

One marketer is unlikely to be strong at design, video, copywriting, SEO, paid media and analytics all at once. Small businesses often hire a generalist and then pay freelancers to fill the gaps anyway. That's not a failure of the hire. It's just how marketing skills are distributed.

Before hiring, list the skills your marketing needs this year and mark which ones a single person could realistically cover well. If more than half the list is left unmarked, a freelancer or a mix of freelancers may give you better coverage for the same money.

When to hire in-house

  • Marketing needs someone on site every day, such as for events, retail or hospitality content.
  • Your monthly freelancer spend is heading towards what a full-time employee would cost, once you count all the costs above.
  • You need someone who'll learn the business deeply and manage other suppliers.
  • Customer conversations on social media and WhatsApp need a fast reply all day, every day.

When a freelancer is the better choice

  • The work comes in bursts: a launch, a website, a season.
  • You need a specialist skill for a few months, not a generalist for years.
  • Nobody in the business has time to manage a marketing employee properly.
  • You're still testing which channels work, so the job description would be a guess.

Making the two work together

When you do hire, the freelancer doesn't have to leave. A common setup is an in-house coordinator who handles daily content, replies and approvals, with a freelancer who owns strategy, ads or technical work. The handover works best when the accounts were always in the company's name, the brand guide is written down and the monthly report already exists. If you can't write a job description that would keep someone busy and productive for 40 hours a week, you're not ready for a full-time marketer yet.
